3.5. Let’s have a recap!

A revision guide!

Religious vocal music

  • Oratorio: similar to opera, but with a religious theme and without staging or actors. It is a long work.
  • Cantata: similar to an oratorio but shorter.

Secular vocal music

  • Opera: a musicalised theatrical work with sets and costumes.
    • Overture: an opening instrumental piece that signals the start of the performance to the audience.
    • Recitative: spoken sections in which singers imitate the natural rhythm of speech to advance the plot.
    • Arias: the lyrical and emotional heart of the work. They pause the dramatic action so that the soloist can express their deepest feelings with a great display of virtuosity.
    • Choruses: pieces in which the choir takes part to represent collective characters.
    • Interludes: instrumental pieces that allowed for scene changes or dances.

Instrumental music

  • Concerto: a confrontation. Three-part structure (I. Fast – II. Slow – III. Fast)
    • Grosso: a small group of instruments (concertino) versus the orchestra (tutti).
    • Solo: a soloist (or sometimes a duet) versus the orchestra.
  • Suite:
    • Allemande: of German origin, in duple metre and at a moderate tempo. It has a serious, measured character.
    • Courante: of French origin, in triple metre and at a fast tempo. It is lively and features leaping movements.
    • Sarabande: of Spanish (and Latin American) origin, in triple metre and at a very slow tempo. It is unhurried, solemn and majestic.
